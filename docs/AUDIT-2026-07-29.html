Internal audit · Irish greyhound racing

Greyhound Predictor: full audit & profit plan

Prepared 29 July 2026 · repo BigBobbo/2026_greyhounds @ 6f8d8a9 · 90 commits, 47 PRs, dormant since 4 May 2026 · audited by 4 code agents + 2 research agents, verified against the live GRI site

Verdict: keep the platform, fix the model core, build the missing market layer. A rewrite is not warranted.

The skeleton is genuinely good — the scraper targets URL patterns I verified live today, the schema is sensible, the feature library is extensive, and recent work (NaN passthrough, post-race guards, Plackett–Luce ordering) is directionally right. But the app cannot make money as it stands, for three reasons: (1) data leakage means every trained model and every backtest number produced so far is invalid-optimistic; (2) the system never sees a market price before a race — its Kelly staking engine is literally inert on upcoming races and its "value bets" are computed against post-race prices nobody can obtain; (3) the staking/settlement layer has outright bugs (a "Lost" button that can settle place bets as winners; up to 250% of bankroll recommended across one card).

The fix list is surgical, not architectural: ~15 targeted repairs plus one genuinely new subsystem (a pre-race odds + market-blending + bet-sheet layer). All of it fits in the days before and during the 7-day evaluation.

Section 2

How the app works today

A FastAPI backend (SQLite, 13 tables, 79 endpoints) scrapes grireland.ie results and race cards with httpx + BeautifulSoup. A feature store materializes 46 user-configurable features per dog per race; five more built-in layers (race context, ELO, head-to-head, pace shape, ~180 race-relative columns) are computed at train and predict time. Four trainers (XGBoost, LightGBM, LambdaRank, sklearn) with Optuna tuning produce Platt-calibrated win probabilities; a Monte-Carlo Plackett–Luce layer with Henery discounting derives place/forecast/trio probabilities; a quarter-Kelly staking function produces bet recommendations; a React frontend drives everything manually, plus two cron jobs scrape results nightly and per-model schedules can predict upcoming cards each morning.

Operationally: production runs on Railway with the database and model artifacts on a persistent volume — neither exists in this repo, and there is no backup or export path. Development stopped 4 May 2026, so the production data and models are ~3 months stale. The betting ledger has no way to place a bet from the UI, and settlement is a manual per-bet click that never consults the scraped results.

What deserves credit: the two prior in-repo audits (PREDICTION_FEATURE_AUDIT.md, FEATURE_SUGGESTIONS.md) correctly diagnosed the train/serve gap on post-race features and shipped real fixes (hard guards, NaN passthrough for GBMs, per-entry data-completeness scores). The failure mode is that those fixes were name-list based and stopped one layer short of the root causes below.

Section 3

Findings

Six agents produced ~100 distinct findings. Below are the ones that block profit, grouped by theme and ordered by how directly they cost money. The long tail is in the appendix.

A · Model correctness — every past backtest number is invalid

CriticalPopulation aggregates are computed over all time — including the future

Trap win rates, trainer stats, sire stats, and — worst — the speed-figure normalization baselines are built from the entire table with no date filter, so every historical speed figure is normalized against races that hadn't happened yet, including the target race itself. This inflates cross-validation, and at predict time the same tables are rebuilt over a different population, adding train/serve drift on top. Six speed-figure features plus ~15 race-relative derivatives inherit the contamination.

race_features.py:610-860
CriticalWalk-forward CV — the main anti-leakage control — silently never runs

Training rows are materialized newest-first (ORDER BY race_date DESC) and never re-sorted, but the fold generator documents that it requires ascending order. Fed descending data, its embargo check is true for every fold, every fold is skipped, and training silently falls back to a single split. Optuna's "walk-forward" model selection has therefore never actually walked forward. The same unsorted order means LambdaRank's race groups rest on incidental SQLite row order.

dataset_builder.py:106-125 dataset_builder.py:505-539 training_service.py:584-590
CriticalA seeded feature leaks the closing market price past the leakage guard

The post-race guard is a hard-coded list of feature names. The user-selectable code feature current_race_sp reads the current race's SP — published only after the race — and is not on the list. Any experiment that includes it trains on the closing market price, produces a spectacular backtest, passes the strict guard, and gets NaN at serve time. Two more escapees: weight_pct_of_career_avg (on-the-night weigh-in) and trap_bias_deviation_going (post-race going).

seed_features.py:188-200 race_features.py:1690-1698 feature_availability.py:25-50
CriticalServed probabilities don't sum to 1 per race — and feed Kelly anyway

The LambdaRank path deliberately skips re-normalization after Platt calibration, so per-race win probabilities can sum to 0.8 or 1.3. Those raw numbers go straight into edge computation, Kelly stakes, confidence scores, and the Plackett–Luce combo layer (which then silently renormalizes — so the win column and the forecast/trio columns on the same screen are on different scales). Pointwise models are normalized at serve but not in the backtest, so the backtested edge is not the edge you bet. Any systematic scale error maps directly into wrong stake sizes.

lambdarank_trainer.py:231-235 prediction_service.py:590-599 race_ordering.py:88
HighEvaluation selects on noise and reports theater metrics

The autoresearch loop picks "improvements" by test-set Kelly ROI across hundreds of trials — a maximum over noise (±5pp standard error on ~1,200 test races), with no confidence intervals anywhere. Optuna's betting objectives score uncalibrated probabilities while final metrics use calibrated ones. Headline "accuracy" is a 0.5-threshold binary accuracy on a 17% base rate (≈83% for predicting nobody wins). The final model is never refit on the most recent 15% of data — the races most like the ones we'll bet.

B · Market blindness — the profit-critical gap

CriticalNo pre-race odds exist anywhere in the system

The only odds ever ingested are starting prices scraped from results pages after the race. On every upcoming race, sp_decimal is NULL and the Kelly engine returns {"bet": false, "reason": "no_odds"} — the staking engine is inert on exactly the races you'd bet. The Betfair integration is three unused config variables; the odds_snapshots table has a full read path, a UI toggle defaulted ON, and no writer. Manually entered odds aren't even persisted.

prediction_service.py:314 config.py:16-18 app/models/odds.py
CriticalBacktests bet at a price that cannot be obtained

Every "value bet", every best-bets list, and the entire betting simulation use the final post-race SP as the assumed execution price. Betting at SP-known-in-advance is a fantasy; real edges at real prices are systematically smaller. No commission, no overround haircut, no minimum-odds or liquidity constraint is modeled anywhere.

evaluation.py:121-284 api/predictions.py:1169-1257
HighNo market blending — the one architecture research says separates winners from losers

Nothing combines model probability with market probability. Benter's published result (and its modern replications) is blunt: raw model probabilities are biased specifically on the bets the model wants to make; the fix is a second-stage logit on (log model prob + log market implied prob), fitted out-of-sample. One replication reports 17.5% Kelly returns for the two-step vs 0.96% for the one-step on the same model. The model also trains with zero market features by default, yet is evaluated against the market — a structurally unprofitable setup.

dataset_builder.py:681-758 (built, off)

C · Staking & settlement — bugs that lose money directly

CriticalThe "Lost" button can settle place/show bets as winners

The dashboard's Lost button posts actual_position: 2; the API treats position ≤ 2 as a win for place bets (≤ 3 for show). Clicking "Lost" on a place bet credits the bankroll. Also: a winning bet with NULL odds is booked as a loss; place bets pay full win odds (no place terms); combo bets can't be settled from the UI at all; and nothing ever reconciles the ledger against the scraped results sitting in the same database.

D · Data quality — silent corruption and dead columns

CriticalResults can be written to the wrong dog

Entries are upserted by (race, trap), and the update path never touches dog_id. Standard workflow — card scraped days ahead, reserve dog substituted on the night — attaches the actual result (position, time, SP, comment) to the dog that didn't run, poisoning both the training label and both dogs' form history. Dogs are also matched by name alone with no unique constraint: greyhound names recur across generations, merging different dogs into one blended history.

Scale of the opportunity: Ireland runs ~27 meetings and ~280 races per week across ~20 tracks (busiest: Friday with 8 meetings; Shelbourne Park races Thu/Fri/Sat). Cards publish 2–5 days ahead.

Strategy evidence

Channel economics decide everything

  • Retail bookmaker win books: ~120–130% overround — needs a >20% edge to break even.
  • Betfair exchange: ~101% book + 2–5% commission — the only channel where a realistic 2–10% model edge survives.
  • Tote Ireland takeout: ~20% win, ~25% place, ~30% exotics — beatable only with a large edge, but tiny stakes don't move the pool, which is a €100 bettor's one structural advantage.

The Benter blend is non-negotiable

  • Model probabilities are biased precisely on the bets the model likes; blending log(model prob) with log(market prob) in a second-stage logit, fitted out-of-sample, corrects it.
  • Published replication: 17.5% Kelly return two-step vs 1.0% one-step, same underlying model.
  • The right test of our model isn't accuracy — it's whether it adds pseudo-R² on top of the market (Benter's ΔR² test).

Greyhound-market specifics

  • BSP is near-efficient: backing every favourite loses ≈ the commission (−0.9% over 950k races). Naive strategies all lose.
  • Favorite-longshot bias runs in reverse in dog pari-mutuel markets (favourites overbet) — don't import horse-racing priors.
  • Early pace / sectional to the first bend is the most predictive single variable; trap bias is real but track-specific and interacts with seeding.
  • Exotics carry more edge per unit of model quality (Benter: two slightly-negative-EV dogs can combine into a +16% EV forecast), but Irish pools are small and takeout is ~30%.

Kelly with €100

  • Quarter-Kelly or less for an unvalidated model — overestimating edge 2× makes full Kelly growth-negative.
  • Betfair minimum €2 back stake (or €10-payout rule) means only the largest edges are bettable at this bankroll — which aligns with the standard advice anyway (bet rarely, only big edges).
  • Realistic ceiling for a good blended model on exchange: 2–8% ROI on turnover. Anyone promising more over 7 days is describing variance.

The plan

Sequenced so that every day of the evaluation window has the best system available that day. Phases 0–2 are the pre-flight repairs; Phase 3 is the new subsystem; Phase 4 is the daily race-day loop. I can start Phase 0 immediately on approval.

0

Rebuild the data foundation · day 1 · runs unattended

Stand the backend up in this environment and re-scrape GRI from scratch: ~2 years of results across all tracks (~16k requests, ~5h at a polite 1/s), then a new dog-profile scraper to fill the dead columns — sectionals, running positions, seeding, whelp date — for every dog seen. Fix the scraper's corruption bugs first (wrong-dog upsert, permanent-first-write guards, margin parsing, retries with backoff). A fresh scrape is honestly cleaner than the production DB, which has been accumulating the wrong-dog bug for months; if you can export the Railway volume I'll use it for cross-validation of the scrape rather than as the primary source.

1

Fix model correctness, retrain, measure honestly · day 1–2

Sort chronologically and assert race-group integrity; make every population aggregate as-of-date; delete the three leaking features; renormalize probabilities per race after calibration (one softmax, one place, used everywhere); refit final models on train+val; run real walk-forward evaluation with bootstrap confidence intervals; report log-loss vs the SP-implied market baseline (the ΔR² test) as the headline metric instead of accuracy. This tells us — before risking a euro — whether the model genuinely knows anything the market doesn't.

2

Build the market layer · day 2–3

Historical: join Betfair BSP files (Kaggle mirror, or EU egress if available) for realistic execution prices; enable SP features for training (they're legitimate historical inputs). Live: a pre-race odds path — fast manual entry against the day's card at minimum, Playwright-scraped or Betfair-API prices if access allows — persisted to odds_snapshots at last. Then the Benter two-stage blend fitted on held-out races, and a rebuilt backtest that bets at obtainable prices minus commission/takeout, walk-forward, with confidence intervals.

3

Strategy generator + honest ledger · day 3

One Kelly implementation, reading the real config and the real bankroll, with de-vig, commission, a simultaneous solve for same-race candidates, and hard caps (per-bet, per-race, per-day, drawdown stop). Output: a daily bet sheet where every recommendation is odds-conditional — "back Dog X to win if you can get ≥ 3.2" — because that's robust to us not having a live feed at the moment the bet is placed, and executable by your father in seconds from a betting app. Forecast/trio prices from the Henery layer with exponents fitted to Irish data. Auto-settlement from scraped results; append-only prediction log so the evaluation is tamper-proof; fix the settlement bugs.

4

The live week · each race day

Morning: scrape today's cards + latest dog form, predict, blend against whatever prices are obtainable, publish the day's bet sheet with stakes. Evening: scrape results, auto-settle, update the bankroll and a running evaluation page. Continuously: every prediction logged before the off, every result reconciled after — so at the end of 7 days the P&L is auditable end-to-end.

Section 6

The €100 week: proposed betting policy

Division of labour: the system (and I) produce a daily bet sheet; your father executes it from Ireland. That drives the design — every recommendation must be self-contained and checkable in seconds by someone standing at a betting app, not a terminal: dog name, track, race time, bet type, stake in euro, and a minimum acceptable price ("back Swift Sydney, Shelbourne 20:14, WIN €4 — only if odds ≥ 3.2"). His being in Ireland also means Betfair, Barking Buzz, and all Irish books are accessible for execution regardless of the geo-blocks this server sees. The policy itself, to be finalized after Phase 2's backtest:

  • Channel order of preference: Betfair exchange (if you have an account) → fixed-odds books at published early prices → Barking Buzz tote for exotics. The required edge roughly doubles at each step right.
  • Bet types: primarily win singles in the 2.5–8.0 price band (where the model has the most room and dog markets overbet favourites); selective forecasts/trios only where the blended edge is large enough to clear ~30% tote takeout — evidence says exotics are where model edge concentrates, and €1–2 stakes don't dilute small pools.
  • Staking: quarter-Kelly on the blended probability, €2 minimum / €5 initial maximum per bet, at most 2 bets per race after a simultaneous solve, daily exposure cap 25% of current bankroll, hard stop for the day at −20%.
  • Cadence: roughly 3–8 bets/day across ~40 daily races, concentrated on meetings with the best price availability (Shelbourne Thu–Sat, Friday's 8-meeting card). No bet is ever forced — a day with no qualifying edge is a €0 day, and that's the system working.
  • Honesty clause: with €100 over one week, variance dominates any realistic edge — a genuinely +5% EV week still loses money ~45% of the time. The policy maximizes expected log-growth and auditability; it cannot promise a green week. What it can promise is that every bet had positive expected value at the price taken, with the reasoning logged before the off.
